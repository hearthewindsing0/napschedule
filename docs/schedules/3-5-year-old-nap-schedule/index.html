Preschoolers need about 10 to 13 hours of sleep in 24 hours. Some still take a daily nap; others have stopped and get all their sleep at night. Both can be perfectly normal.

When the nap goes, it's worth keeping a daily quiet time of 30 to 60 minutes. Quiet time gives your child (and you) a recharge, and it's an easy way to bring back a nap on days they really need one.

Following Babywise? The same predictable rhythm carries on into toddlerhood: consistent wake-up, meals, naps and bedtime at about the same times every day. The Ezzos cover this stage in On Becoming Pretoddlerwise and On Becoming Toddlerwise.

Tips for this age

  • Signs a nap is still needed: meltdowns in the late afternoon, falling asleep in the car, or crankiness before dinner.
  • Signs a nap may be ready to go: taking a long time to fall asleep at nap or bedtime, or bedtime drifting past 8:30–9:00 PM on nap days.
  • Without a nap, move bedtime earlier (often 6:30–7:30 PM) for a while.
  • Make quiet time predictable: same place, same time, a basket of books or quiet toys, and a timer or toddler clock.

What's next

By kindergarten, most children no longer nap and sleep about 10 to 12 hours a night.

Frequently asked questions

What age do kids stop napping?

Most children stop napping between ages 3 and 5. A small number nap a little longer, and that's fine too.

Should a 4 year old take a nap?

Only if they need one. If your 4 year old naps and still falls asleep easily at a reasonable bedtime, there's no reason to stop.
