Newborn (0–6 weeks) nap schedule

A realistic newborn nap schedule for 0–6 weeks: wake windows, how many naps, total sleep, and a sample day you can adapt.

Total sleep (24 hrs)14–17 hours
Naps per day4–7 (irregular)
Wake windows35–60 minutes
Daytime sleep6–8 hours
Typical bedtimeNo set bedtime yet (often 9–11 PM)

In the first six weeks, there isn't really a schedule, and that's normal. Newborns sleep in short stretches around the clock because their stomachs are tiny and their internal body clock (circadian rhythm) hasn't developed yet. Most newborns sleep somewhere between 14 and 17 hours in 24 hours, spread across many naps and night stretches of two to four hours.

What you can do now is watch wake windows. Most newborns can only stay comfortably awake for about 35 to 60 minutes, and that includes the time it takes to feed, burp, and change them. Getting them back down before they become overtired makes everything easier.

Tips for this age

  • Feed on demand. At this age, feeding drives the day, not the clock.
  • Expose your baby to daylight and normal household noise during the day, and keep nights dark, quiet, and boring. This helps their body clock develop.
  • Watch for early sleepy cues: staring off, a red tint around the eyebrows, slower movements, or the first yawn.
  • Contact naps, stroller naps, and naps in a carrier are all fine. Short, messy naps are developmentally normal.
  • Always follow safe sleep basics for any sleep you can't supervise: alone, on the back, in a crib or bassinet with a firm flat mattress.

What's next

Somewhere around 6–8 weeks, many babies start staying awake a little longer and the day begins to fall into a loose rhythm of four to five naps.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a newborn be awake between naps?

Most newborns do best with wake windows of about 35 to 60 minutes, counting feeding and diaper changes. Toward six weeks, many can stretch closer to an hour.

Is it OK if my newborn only takes short naps?

Yes. Naps of 20 to 45 minutes are very common in the newborn period. Longer, more predictable naps usually develop after 3 to 4 months.

Should I wake my newborn to feed?

In the early weeks many pediatricians recommend not letting a newborn go more than about 3 hours during the day without feeding, especially until they regain their birth weight. Ask your pediatrician what's right for your baby.

This site shares general information, not medical advice. Every child is different. Talk with your pediatrician about your child's sleep, especially if you have concerns about health, growth, or breathing.
