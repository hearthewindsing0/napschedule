By 19 to 24 months, nearly all toddlers are on one nap. The nap typically starts after lunch and lasts 1.5 to 2.5 hours, with wake windows of about 5 to 6 hours before and after.

Growing independence, language explosions, and big feelings can all lead to stalling and protests at naptime. Many toddlers go through an 18-month or 2-year sleep regression, which is usually about testing boundaries more than needing less sleep.

Following Babywise? The same predictable rhythm carries on into toddlerhood: consistent wake-up, meals, naps and bedtime at about the same times every day. The Ezzos cover this stage in On Becoming Pretoddlerwise and On Becoming Toddlerwise.

Tips for this age

  • Keep the pre-nap routine short and the same every day: books, sleep sack, song, lights out.
  • Offer limited choices ("this book or that one?") to give a sense of control.
  • If your toddler talks or plays for a while before falling asleep, that's fine as long as they eventually rest.
  • Avoid naps that run past about 3:00–3:30 PM, which can push bedtime late.

What's next

One nap continues for most children into the preschool years.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a 2 year old nap?

Most 2 year olds nap 1 to 2.5 hours, once a day, after lunch.

My 20 month old is refusing naps. Are they ready to drop it?

Very unlikely. Most children need a daily nap until at least age 3. Nap refusal at this age is usually a phase or a sign the schedule needs a small adjustment.
