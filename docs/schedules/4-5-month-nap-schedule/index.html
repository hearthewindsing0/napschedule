4–5 months nap schedule

At four to five months, many babies go through a permanent shift in how they sleep, often called the 4-month sleep regression. Sleep becomes more adult-like, with lighter stages, which can mean more wake-ups at first. The upside: this is when a more predictable nap schedule usually becomes possible.

Most babies this age take three or four naps with wake windows of about 1.5 to 2.25 hours. Four-month-olds often still need the fourth short nap; by five months many are ready to drop to three.

Tips for this age

  • Aim for a consistent morning wake time; it sets the rhythm for the whole day.
  • The midday nap is often the longest. Protect it with a dark room and white noise.
  • If your baby fights the last nap, try a short 20–30 minute catnap on the go.
  • Bedtime around 7–8 PM works for most babies this age, but an earlier bedtime after a day of short naps is fine.
  • Stop swaddling as soon as your baby shows signs of trying to roll. A wearable blanket is a good next step.

What's next

When your baby consistently refuses the fourth nap or bedtime gets pushed too late, it's usually time to move to three naps, most often between 5 and 6 months.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a 4 month old nap?

Total daytime sleep is usually around 3 to 4 hours across 3 or 4 naps. Individual naps can range from 30 minutes to 2 hours.

When should a 4 month old go to bed?

Most do well with a bedtime between 7 and 8 PM, roughly 1.5 to 2 hours after the end of the last nap.
