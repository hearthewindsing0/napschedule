When do kids stop napping?

Most kids stop napping between ages 3 and 5. Learn the signs your child is ready to drop the nap and how to replace it with quiet time.

Most children stop napping somewhere between ages 3 and 5. There's a wide range of normal: some 3 year olds no longer nap, and some 5 year olds still benefit from one.

Signs your child may be ready to stop napping

  • They regularly take a long time to fall asleep at nap time, or don't fall asleep at all, without getting cranky later.
  • On nap days, bedtime drifts very late (past 8:30–9:00 PM) or turns into a battle.
  • They're in a good mood through the late afternoon on days without a nap.
  • They sleep about 11 or more hours at night on no-nap days.

Signs they still need it

  • Meltdowns or clumsiness in the late afternoon
  • Falling asleep in the car or on the couch
  • Waking more at night or very early on no-nap days

How to drop the nap gently

  1. Shorten it first. Cap the nap at 45–60 minutes and see whether bedtime improves.
  2. Try every-other-day naps for a few weeks.
  3. Replace nap with quiet time: 30–60 minutes in their room with books or quiet toys.
  4. Move bedtime earlier, often by 30–60 minutes, at least until they adjust.

Frequently asked questions

What age do most kids stop napping?

Most children stop napping between ages 3 and 5.

Is it bad if my 3 year old doesn't nap?

Not necessarily. If they get enough sleep at night (usually 10 to 13 hours total in 24 hours) and are in good spirits during the day, they may simply have outgrown the nap.

This site shares general information, not medical advice. Every child is different. Talk with your pediatrician about your child's sleep, especially if you have concerns about health, growth, or breathing.
