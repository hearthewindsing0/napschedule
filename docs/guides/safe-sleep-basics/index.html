Safe sleep basics for naps

A quick, plain-language checklist of safe sleep basics for babies under one, following the American Academy of Pediatrics recommendations.

Naps count as sleep, so the same safety recommendations apply during the day as at night. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends the following for babies under one year. Talk with your pediatrician about anything specific to your baby.

The safe sleep checklist

  • Back to sleep, every sleep. Place your baby on their back for every nap and night. Once they can roll both ways on their own, you don't need to roll them back.
  • Firm, flat, level surface. A crib, bassinet, or play yard that meets current safety standards, with a fitted sheet.
  • Nothing else in the sleep space. No pillows, blankets, bumpers, positioners, or soft toys.
  • Room-share, don't bed-share. The AAP recommends your baby sleep in your room, on a separate surface, ideally for at least the first six months.
  • Avoid inclined sleepers and letting your baby sleep for long periods in car seats, swings, or bouncers outside the car. Move them to a flat surface when you can.
  • Don't overheat. Dress your baby in no more than one layer more than an adult would be comfortable in. A wearable blanket (sleep sack) replaces loose blankets.
  • Stop swaddling as soon as your baby shows signs of trying to roll.
  • Offer a pacifier at sleep times if your baby takes one (once breastfeeding is established, if breastfeeding).
  • Smoke-free environment, during pregnancy and after birth.

Naps on the go

Car seat, stroller, and carrier naps happen, and that's OK while you're supervising and baby is positioned correctly. In a carrier, keep your baby's face visible and their chin off their chest. When you arrive, transfer to a flat, safe sleep space if they'll be sleeping for a while.

For full details, see the AAP's safe sleep resources at HealthyChildren.org.

Frequently asked questions

Can my baby nap in a swing or bouncer?

The AAP advises against routine sleep in sitting devices like swings and bouncers. If your baby falls asleep in one, move them to a firm, flat sleep surface.

When can my baby sleep with a blanket?

The AAP recommends keeping blankets and soft objects out of the sleep space for at least the first 12 months. A wearable blanket is a safe alternative.

This site shares general information, not medical advice. Every child is different. Talk with your pediatrician about your child's sleep, especially if you have concerns about health, growth, or breathing.
