Sleep regressions by age

What sleep regressions are, when they usually happen (4, 8–10, 12, 18 months and 2 years), how long they last, and what actually helps.

A sleep regression is a period when a baby or toddler who was sleeping fairly well suddenly starts waking more at night, fighting naps, or taking shorter naps. Regressions usually line up with big developmental leaps and typically last two to six weeks.

"Regression" is a slightly misleading word: your child isn't going backwards. Their brain and body are changing, and sleep is often the first place you notice it.

Common sleep regressions

4 months

The best-known regression, and the only one that reflects a permanent change in sleep structure. Babies start cycling through lighter and deeper stages like adults do, so they surface more often between cycles. Read the full 4-month regression guide.

8–10 months

Crawling, pulling to stand, and separation anxiety all arrive around now. Babies may practice standing in the crib or protest when you leave the room. This is also near the time many babies move from three naps to two.

12 months

Often shows up as refusing one of the two naps. Most of the time it's a temporary phase, not a sign your child is ready for one nap. Hold steady for a couple of weeks before making changes.

18 months

Language, independence, and teething (molars and canines) can combine into a challenging few weeks. Expect protests at bedtime and naptime.

2 years

Big life changes like a new sibling, potty training, or a toddler bed, plus fears and a growing imagination, can disrupt sleep. Clear, consistent limits help a lot.

What helps during any regression

  • Keep the routine the same. Predictability is reassuring when everything else is changing.
  • Offer lots of practice time for new skills during the day.
  • Use an earlier bedtime to make up for short or skipped naps.
  • Avoid starting habits you don't want to keep, if you can. Comfort your child, but try to return to your usual approach once things settle.
  • Rule out illness. Ear infections, fever, or reflux can look like a regression. Check with your pediatrician if you're unsure.

Frequently asked questions

How long do sleep regressions last?

Most last about 2 to 6 weeks. If disrupted sleep lasts much longer, look at the schedule (it may need adjusting for age) or talk with your pediatrician.

Does every baby have sleep regressions?

No. Some babies sail through these ages with barely a blip, while others are more sensitive to developmental changes.

This site shares general information, not medical advice. Every child is different. Talk with your pediatrician about your child's sleep, especially if you have concerns about health, growth, or breathing.
