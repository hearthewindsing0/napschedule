Wake windows by age

A simple wake window chart from newborn to 5 years, plus how to use wake windows to plan naps without watching the clock all day.

A wake window is the stretch of time your baby is awake between one sleep and the next. Because babies can only handle so much awake time before they become overtired, wake windows are one of the most useful tools for planning naps, especially in the first year.

How to use wake windows

  1. Start the clock when your baby wakes up, not when you get them out of the crib.
  2. Use the shorter end of the range for the first wake window of the day. Most babies get sleepy fastest in the morning.
  3. Use the longer end before bedtime. The last wake window is usually the longest.
  4. Watch your baby, not just the timer. Yawning, rubbing eyes, staring off, or fussiness mean it's time, even if the window says otherwise.

Signs a wake window is too short or too long

Too short (undertired): your baby takes a long time to fall asleep, chats or plays in the crib, or takes a short nap and wakes happy.

Too long (overtired): your baby is fussy or wired, cries hard at naptime, falls asleep quickly but wakes after 30–45 minutes upset, or has early-morning wakings.

Adjust in small steps of 10–15 minutes and give each change a few days before deciding whether it worked.

Frequently asked questions

What is a wake window?

A wake window is the amount of time a baby or toddler is awake between sleeps, measured from when they wake up until they fall asleep again.

Do wake windows matter after one year?

They still help, but by the time a child is on one nap, a consistent daily schedule usually matters more than precise wake windows.
