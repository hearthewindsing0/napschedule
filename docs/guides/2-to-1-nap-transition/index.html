Moving from 2 naps to 1

Signs your toddler is ready for one nap (usually 13–18 months), a step-by-step plan for the 2 to 1 nap transition, and a sample schedule.

The move from two naps to one usually happens between 13 and 18 months, with 15 months being a very common age. It's one of the bigger transitions, and it can take a few weeks for your toddler to fully adjust.

Don't jump too early

Lots of babies refuse a nap around their first birthday. If your child is under 13 months, a nap strike is more likely a phase than a sign they're ready. Try to keep two naps for another couple of weeks first.

Signs your toddler is ready

  • Refusing either nap most days for two weeks or longer
  • Taking a long time (20+ minutes) to fall asleep for naps
  • A short or skipped afternoon nap after a good morning nap
  • Bedtime battles or early-morning wakings that weren't there before

How to make the switch

  1. Push the morning nap later by 15–30 minutes every few days until it starts around 11:30 AM to 12:30 PM.
  2. Serve lunch just before the nap, which helps hunger not cut it short.
  3. Move bedtime earlier (often to 6:30–7:00 PM) for the first few weeks.
  4. Allow a two-nap day now and then if your toddler is exhausted.
  5. Keep the room dark and boring. A longer single nap needs a good sleep environment.

Frequently asked questions

When do babies go down to one nap?

Most toddlers make the switch between 13 and 18 months.

How long does the 2 to 1 nap transition take?

Usually 2 to 6 weeks. An earlier bedtime during this time helps prevent overtiredness.
