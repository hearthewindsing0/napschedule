The 4-month sleep regression

Why the 4-month sleep regression happens, signs to watch for, how long it lasts, and practical steps to help your baby (and you) sleep better.

Somewhere between about 3 and 5 months, many babies who were sleeping in decent stretches start waking every one to two hours and taking shorter naps. This is the 4-month sleep regression, and it's caused by a real, permanent change in how your baby sleeps.

Why it happens

Newborns spend a large share of sleep in "active" sleep and drop into deep sleep easily. Around four months, sleep matures into distinct cycles with lighter and deeper stages, more like adult sleep. At the end of each cycle (roughly every 45 minutes during naps and every one to two hours at night), your baby comes close to waking. If they fell asleep being fed, rocked, or held, they may need that same help to get back to sleep.

Signs of the 4-month regression

  • More frequent night wakings after a period of longer stretches
  • Naps of 30–45 minutes
  • More fussiness and harder settling
  • Increased appetite (often coincides with a growth spurt)

What helps

  1. Move to a 4-month-appropriate schedule. Wake windows of about 1.5–2 hours and 3–4 naps. See the Babywise 4–5 month schedule.
  2. Make the room sleep-friendly: dark for naps and nights, with steady white noise.
  3. Use a consistent, short routine before every sleep.
  4. Practice putting your baby down drowsy but awake for at least one sleep a day. Falling asleep independently is what helps babies link cycles.
  5. Use an earlier bedtime if naps were short.
  6. Transition out of the swaddle if your baby is showing any signs of rolling.

How long does it last?

The fussiest part often lasts two to six weeks. Because the underlying change in sleep is permanent, the "end" of the regression is really your baby learning to handle their new sleep cycles. Many families find that this is the point where sleep starts to get noticeably better.

Frequently asked questions

When does the 4 month sleep regression start?

It can start anywhere from about 3 to 5 months. Babies born early often hit it a little later, based on their adjusted age.

Can you prevent the 4 month regression?

You can't prevent the change in sleep cycles, but an age-appropriate schedule, a good sleep environment, and some practice falling asleep independently can make it much smoother.
