Why is my baby taking short naps?

Why babies take 30–45 minute naps, when short naps are normal, and practical fixes for catnapping after 5–6 months.

If your baby wakes up after 30 to 45 minutes like clockwork, you're seeing the end of a single sleep cycle. Short naps are extremely common and are often completely normal under about 5 to 6 months. Many babies learn to connect sleep cycles for longer naps between 4 and 7 months.

Common causes of short naps

  • Not enough sleep pressure. The wake window before the nap was too short.
  • Overtiredness. The wake window was too long, and stress hormones make it hard to stay asleep.
  • Too much light or noise. Daytime sounds and sunlight wake babies at the light end of a cycle.
  • Sleep associations. If your baby fell asleep while feeding or being rocked, they may need that again to fall back asleep.
  • Hunger, especially if a feed was small or rushed.
  • Developmental leaps and new skills like rolling or crawling.

What to try

  1. Check the wake window against our wake window chart, then adjust by 10–15 minutes.
  2. Darken the room as much as you can and use continuous white noise.
  3. Wait a few minutes before going in when your baby wakes early. Some babies resettle on their own.
  4. Offer a full feed after the nap, not right before, if your baby tends to fall asleep eating.
  5. Be consistent for at least a week before deciding something isn't working.

If short naps are the only issue and your baby is otherwise happy and sleeping well at night, it may not need fixing at all.

Frequently asked questions

Are 30 minute naps OK for a baby?

Under about 5 to 6 months, yes, short naps are very common. Later, if short naps leave your baby cranky, adjusting wake windows and the sleep environment often helps.

When do babies start taking longer naps?

Most babies start linking sleep cycles for longer naps between about 4 and 7 months, often starting with the midday nap.
