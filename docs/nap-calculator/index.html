How to use these times

For babies under 12 months, each row follows the Babywise order: feed, waketime, then nap. Start the day with the first feed at the same time each morning, and the rest of the day tends to fall into place. Waketime lengths are our estimates within the book's feed spacing. Real babies vary, so treat this as a plan, not a rule.

Frequently asked questions

How does the Babywise calculator work?

For babies under 12 months, it lays out eat-wake-sleep cycles from your first feed of the day, using the feed spacing and number of naps described in On Becoming Babywise for your baby's age. For toddlers, it uses typical awake times and nap lengths.

What if my baby is hungry early?

Feed your baby. The times are a plan for the day, not a rule. Babywise itself says to feed a hungry baby, and the AAP recommends feeding on hunger cues.

What if a nap runs short or long?

Start the next feed at about the planned time if you can, or shift the rest of the day by the same amount. Consistency over several days matters more than any single nap.

About Babywise and feeding schedules: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends feeding babies when they show hunger cues, and in 1998 it raised concerns that strict schedule-based feeding can lead to poor weight gain, dehydration and low milk supply. If you use a Babywise-style routine, treat feeding times as flexible, feed your baby whenever they are hungry, and keep up with weight checks at your pediatrician.
