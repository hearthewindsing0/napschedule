How the Babywise routine works

Babywise organizes the day into repeating feed, waketime, sleep cycles. Instead of feeding a baby to sleep, you feed after they wake, enjoy some awake time, then put them down for a nap. The authors call this parent-directed feeding: the parent sets a rhythm, while still watching the baby's hunger cues.

Over the first year, feeds gradually merge. Night feeds go first, then the late-evening feed, then a daytime feed as solids arrive, until your baby is eating family meals with two naps a day.

Signs your baby is eating enough

  • At least 8 feeds a day in the first few weeks, 7 or more by about 5 weeks.
  • Plenty of wet diapers (about 6 or more a day after the first week) with pale urine.
  • Regular stools and steady weight gain at checkups.
  • A baby who seems satisfied after feeds and is alert during waketime.

If any of these are off, feed more often and call your pediatrician.

The Babywise books

  • On Becoming Babywise: The original book behind the eat-wake-sleep routine, covering birth to about 5 months: full feedings, the feed-wake-sleep cycle and how feeds merge over time.
  • On Becoming Babywise Book Two: The follow-up for roughly 5 to 15 months: solids, naps, the move to fewer feeds and early toddler routines.
  • On Becoming Pretoddlerwise: The Ezzos' book for the 12 to 18 month stretch, including the transition to one nap.
  • On Becoming Toddlerwise: Routines, naps and structure for toddlers from about 18 months to 3 years.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Babywise method?

Babywise is a parent-directed routine from the book On Becoming Babywise by Gary Ezzo and Robert Bucknam, MD. Each part of the day follows the same order: feed, waketime, then sleep. Feeds are spaced roughly 2.5 to 3 hours apart in the early weeks, and feeds gradually merge as the baby grows.

When do Babywise babies sleep through the night?

The authors say many babies on the routine sleep through the night somewhere around 8 to 12 weeks. Many healthy babies take longer, and night feeds should continue as long as your baby is hungry.

Is Babywise safe?

The AAP recommends feeding on hunger cues and has warned that rigid schedules can cause poor weight gain and low milk supply. If you follow a Babywise-style routine, keep it flexible, feed your baby when hungry, and track weight gain with your pediatrician.

About Babywise and feeding schedules: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends feeding babies when they show hunger cues, and in 1998 it raised concerns that strict schedule-based feeding can lead to poor weight gain, dehydration and low milk supply. If you use a Babywise-style routine, treat feeding times as flexible, feed your baby whenever they are hungry, and keep up with weight checks at your pediatrician.
