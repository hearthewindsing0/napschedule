Weeks 1–2 eat-wake-sleep schedule

What a Babywise-style day looks like in the first two weeks: feeds about every 2.5 hours, 5–6 naps, and the signs your newborn is eating enough.

Very little beyond the feed itself

Naps, total sleep and feed counts are from On Becoming Babywise (25th Anniversary Edition) by Gary Ezzo and Robert Bucknam, MD, summarized in our own words. Waketime is our estimate.

In the first two weeks, the Babywise approach is mostly about full feedings and a gentle order to the day: feed, a few minutes awake for burping and a diaper change, then sleep. The book calls this the feed-wake-sleep cycle. At this age, "waketime" is short, because a newborn spends most of their awake time eating.

Babywise suggests feeding roughly every 2.5 hours during the day, and not letting more than about 3 hours pass between daytime feeds in the first weeks. Expect 8 to 10 feeds in 24 hours, including night feeds.

Tips for this stage

  • Aim for full feeds rather than snacking. Keep a sleepy newborn gently awake while eating (undress them a little, tickle their feet).
  • Feed your baby if they show hunger cues before the clock says it's time. Babywise itself says the clock is a guide, not the boss.
  • Watch the diapers. In week 1, look for at least 8 feeds a day and an increasing number of wet diapers; by the end of week 1, about 6 or more wet diapers a day is typical.
  • Don't try to stretch nights yet. Night feeds are expected at this age.

About Babywise and feeding schedules: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends feeding babies when they show hunger cues, and in 1998 it raised concerns that strict schedule-based feeding can lead to poor weight gain, dehydration and low milk supply. If you use a Babywise-style routine, treat feeding times as flexible, feed your baby whenever they are hungry, and keep up with weight checks at your pediatrician.
