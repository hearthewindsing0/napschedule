Weeks 6–7 eat-wake-sleep schedule

Babywise-style day for weeks 6–7: about 3 hours between feeds, 4–6 naps, and how the night starts to stretch.

Naps, total sleep and feed counts are from On Becoming Babywise (25th Anniversary Edition) by Gary Ezzo and Robert Bucknam, MD, summarized in our own words. Waketime is our estimate.

Around weeks 6 and 7, many babies on an eat-wake-sleep routine begin stretching their longest sleep at night. Babywise describes this as the first of several "merges," where two feeds gradually become one as your baby takes more at each feeding.

Daytime feeds settle at roughly 3 hours apart, with a late-evening feed before the longest night stretch.

Tips for this stage

  • Let your baby, not the calendar, decide when a night feed drops. If they wake hungry, feed them.
  • Keep night feeds quiet and dark so they stay all business.
  • Total feeds should still be around 7–8 in 24 hours. If they drop well below that, talk with your pediatrician.

About Babywise and feeding schedules: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends feeding babies when they show hunger cues, and in 1998 it raised concerns that strict schedule-based feeding can lead to poor weight gain, dehydration and low milk supply. If you use a Babywise-style routine, treat feeding times as flexible, feed your baby whenever they are hungry, and keep up with weight checks at your pediatrician.
